Flower Care — help your blooms last longer

Simple care steps dramatically increase vase life. The advice below is written for anyone who receives a bouquet — whether a delicate posy of ranunculus or a dramatic armful of sunflowers.

  1. Trim stems — cut one to two centimeters at a 45° angle before placing stems in water. This increases water uptake and prevents stems from sitting flat against the bottom of the vase.
  2. Use clean water — refill the vase every two days with fresh, lukewarm water and a tiny amount of the flower food provided. Clean the vase between changes to remove bacterial growth.
  3. Remove foliage below the waterline — leaves in the water rot quickly and create bacteria which shortens vase life.
  4. Keep cool — flowers last longer away from direct sunlight, heat sources and ripening fruit. If possible, place arrangements in a cool location overnight.
  5. Re-trim and rearrange — for long-lasting centerpieces, re-trim stems every 3–4 days and reposition fading blooms to the back while trimming the stems to balance the design.

For specific flowers: tulips continue to grow in the vase and may need to be re-trimmed more often; lilies often require removal of pollen sacs to prevent staining; eucalyptus benefits from occasional misting to keep leaves supple.
